RF Regions and Regulations

Reference·Audience: All personas·Use: regional power and certification lookup

The region codes and frequency bands below are indicative and are not carried in the IOTC MQTT API contract — the contract's currentRegion response declares field names, types, descriptions and examples (country, regulatoryStandard, maxTxPowerSupported, channelData, …); it defines no band edges and no per-country table of power ceilings. Country approvals and bands change. Before deploying to a new country, confirm the current band, power ceiling, and certification status against the Zebra regulatory portal and the device's regulatory label.

The reader operates in a single regulatory region that fixes its UHF channel set, frequency-hopping behavior, Listen Before Talk (LBT) requirement, and transmit-power ceiling. No MQTT command sets the region; the get_current_region reference names 123RFID Desktop as the tool that sets it, at bootstrap. Read the ceiling from the reader itself. Region power is reported in dBm, while transmitPower is declared in centi-dBm. Confirm the setting's scale on the firmware before comparing it with the ceiling; do not infer it from a raw number.

This appendix is the lookup table for that workflow: general UHF region/band background, the exact shape of the get_current_region response, its field and response-code semantics, and where to find the authoritative per-country certification status.

Regional UHF Allocations (General Context)​

The table below is general UHF RFID background, not values defined by the IOTC API. The currentRegion schema does not define a list of region codes, regulatory domains, frequency bands, or per-region LBT/hopping flags. It returns only the per-device fields country, regulatoryStandard, frequencyHopping, lbtEnabled, maxTxPowerSupported, minTxPowerSupported, and channelData — both country and regulatoryStandard are free-form strings, not enums. Treat the rows below as indicative industry context only; the device reports its actual operating channels in the channelData array (see The get_current_region response below), not a band range, and the authoritative per-country allocation, power ceiling, and certification status are maintained at the Zebra regulatory portal and on the device's regulatory label.

Broadly, a deployment region fixes a UHF Gen2 frequency band and whether Listen Before Talk and frequency hopping apply:

Example regionApprox. UHF band (MHz)LBTFreq. hopping
United States902–928NoYes
European Union (ETSI)865.6–867.6YesNo (4-channel ETSI EN 302 208)
Japan916.7–920.9YesYes

Band edges and approvals change and many countries split into low/high sub-bands or impose duty-cycle rules; consult the Zebra regulatory portal for the current authoritative list before deploying to a new country.

SKUs, Regulatory Codes and Default RF Modes​

Mode 19 is an M4_256K dense-reader mode, mode 13 an M4_240K general-purpose mode, and mode 9 an M2_256K mode chosen because Japan's 1 W configuration uses listen-before-talk. What those modes mean for throughput is in Link Profiles.

The regulatory code is not the same as the standard family. The table uses 45 distinct codes, and most name a country as well as a family: FCC_USA, ETSI_EU, JAPAN_FCC_LBT, JAPAN_FCC_1W, JAPAN_FCC_250MW, AUSTRALIA_FCC, BRAZIL_FCC, CHINA_FCC, HONGKONG_FCC, SOUTHKOREA_FCC, TAIWAN_FCC, SINGAPORE_FCC, MALAYSIA_FCC, MACAO_FCC, NEWZEALAND_ETSI, NEWZEALAND_FCC, RUSSIA_ETSI, UKRAINE_ETSI, UKRAINE_ETSI_LICENSE, CAMBODIA_ETSI, LAOS_ETSI, LAOS_FCC, MOROCCO_ETSI, PAKISTAN_ETSI, KAZAKHSTAN_ETSI, LEBANON_ETSI, and the three channel-count variants EU_900M_2_CHANNEL, EU_900M_3_CHANNEL and EU_900M_4_CHANNEL, among others.

Two consequences for a client reading get_current_region:

  • Do not match regulatoryStandard against FCC or ETSI alone. A reader can report a country-qualified code. The contract's own examples show both the short and the qualified form, and which one a given firmware reports is not specified in the available sources.
  • Two regions can share a standard family and differ in behavior, as the three EU channel-count codes do. Read channelData, lbtEnabled and frequencyHopping from the reply rather than inferring them from the standard's name.

currentRegion Field Reference​

All seven currentRegion fields are required in a successful response.

FieldTypeExampleMeaning
frequencyHoppingbooleantrueWhether the radio hops across the channel set. Mandatory in most FFH (frequency-hopping) regions such as the US; ETSI uses fixed channels with LBT instead.
channelDataarray of string["915750", "915250", ...]The channels the reader may operate on, rather than band edges. Each entry is a center frequency expressed as a string in kHz. The worked US/FCC example contains 50 channels.
countrystring"United States"The configured country. The field declaration carries the short form USA as its example, while the shipped reply and every US example the contract carries use the long form United States — both spellings are legal today and neither corroborates the other. There is no ISO-3166 constraint. Treat it as a display string, not an enum.
lbtEnabledbooleanfalseListen Before Talk. Required in ETSI/CE and several Asian regions; the radio senses the channel before transmitting. Affects inventory-start timing. false for FCC.
maxTxPowerSupportednumber30The region's regulatory power ceiling, in dBm. Never exceed the ceiling reported by your reader. Confirm the transmitPower scale on your firmware before comparing the setting with this ceiling. Read range is not a power measurement.
minTxPowerSupportednumber0Regulatory/hardware power floor, declared in dBm on its own field declaration — “measured in dBm”, example 0. Every worked example the contract carries reports 0, matching that example, and no bounds and no relationship to maxTxPowerSupported are declared.
regulatoryStandardstring"FCC"The governing standard. A free-form string with no enum or minimum length. The field example is FCC_USA; the worked US/FCC response carries FCC. Read the value from the device rather than predicting it.

Read the ceiling from the reader itself and confirm the transmitPower scale on the firmware before comparing the two fields. Read range does not prove emitted power. See The regulatory ceiling for the RF-tuning workflow.

Reading channelData​

The documented channelData entries are center frequencies expressed as strings in kHz. For example, 915750 represents 915.75 MHz. The US/FCC example contains 50 entries spanning 902.75 to 927.25 MHz. Read the channel count from your reader; do not assume every region has the same count.

Response Codes​

The response.code field carries the command result. get_current_region has exactly one command-specific code, 3; the two universal codes that every command can return apply to it as well.

CodeMeaningApplies to region workflow
0SuccesscurrentRegion is fully populated.
3Not able to retrieve informationRegion state could not be read at that moment. Retry after a short delay; reboot if it persists.
101Default errorThe catch-all, returned when no more specific code applies. Investigate from the surrounding context.

0 and 101 are universal codes, so they are not specific to this command. The complete IOTC response-code table is in Response Codes, and the per-command view is on get_current_region.

regulatoryStandard Values​

The currentRegion.regulatoryStandard field is a free-form string, with no enum or minimum length. The contract carries two example strings: FCC_USA on the field declaration and FCC in the worked US/FCC reply. Treat it as a display or audit value reported by the reader, rather than a fixed set of allowed values or a certification decision.

regulatoryStandard (reported)Source
FCC_USAThe example on the field's own declaration
FCCThe value the worked US/FCC get_current_region response carries

Zebra's own region table also uses country-qualified codes, a 45-value set of country-qualified codes such as FCC_USA, ETSI_EU and JAPAN_FCC_LBT; see SKUs, Regulatory Codes and Default RF Modes. That is the strongest evidence available that the qualified form is real, and it does not settle which form a given firmware reports in this field. Match on the value you read back, not on a name you expect.

The device's specific certifications and approval status per country are on its regulatory label and on the Zebra product page — they are not carried in the IOTC API response.

Pre-Deployment Compliance Check​

Use this sequence to confirm a reader is compliant before starting inventory:

  1. Send get_current_region and confirm response.code is 0 (a 3 means region state could not be read — retry).
  2. Verify country and regulatoryStandard match the deployment location.
  3. Read maxTxPowerSupported from that reader. Its ceiling is reported in dBm. Confirm the transmitPower scale on the firmware before comparing the values.
  4. Do not derive a transmitPower setting from a reported ceiling until the setting's scale is confirmed. 300 is the schema maximum for transmitPower, rather than a documented region ceiling.
  5. Set a low transmitPower, read it back with get_operating_mode, and raise it in steps. Read range is not a power measurement, so it cannot tell you where to stop: where regulatory margin matters, confirm the power actually applied against Zebra's firmware documentation or an instrument, and treat that confirmation as the stopping rule (see the maxTxPowerSupported row above).
  6. Confirm lbtEnabled and frequencyHopping match the region's expectations (e.g. LBT on for ETSI).
  7. Confirm channelData is non-empty and the channel count matches the region.
